## Research

I came into the Academia and Research world a bit more than 3 years ago. I wanted to do research, inspired, and all those synonyms full of hopefulness and joy. I learned a lot, now I am tired.

## Rant

The rant is not about research perse, that is another topic. It just about how in the last year with LLMs it has become utterly stressful and annoying how the Academia Industry (YES!, IT IS AN INDUSTRY TO JUST PUBLISH PAPERS) has become.  

I have done research, helped publish papers, used LLMs when they weren't "smart". They were unhelpful two years ago, one year ago they could barely breath while swimming, this year, they learned know how to swim. Now everyone relies on them thinking that they are the best tool in the world, when in reality they make mistakes obvious to us who we read papers.

I reviewed papers with no AI help, had to understand the problem, the proposed solution, the methodology, had to find the mistakes. I had to think to give good comments. I had to think to come up with an implementation to my work. I had to think to understand the problem, and to understand which tools I could use to come out with the solution. Nowadays, everything is relied of any AI with the laziest prompt of:

``` 
    Role: You are a cybersecurity reviewer
    Context: Review the attached paper, compare it to the related work.
    Task: Tell me problem it tries to solve, the methodology in detail, tell me if its novel enough
````

The results are always the same: "It is not novel enough" or something along those lines. Then the reviewers will put a familiarity score of "somewhat familiar" and the paper will get rejected for a technicality of AI laziness and you'll get the typical comments of "Elaborate more here, explain here". Meanwhile, methodology-wise it won't have an issue. But reviewers were lazy enough to depend on AI, not read the full paper and then ask AI to find any issue to reject your paper.

## Prediction

Academia is probably the only industry that will see the impacts on reliability of AI and LLMs faster than any other industry in the world. For future researchers, they will become more lazy and at the same time more worried that "AI can do their jobs faster" (at least reviewing and reading papers). They will become lazy to understand papers and problems. They are already facing said problems, but those sentiments and pressure will keep increasing. The ones that stick to the old-school mentality won't be able to succeed because they are taking too long, and the ones that know how to use AI, will get accepted without much issue and not necessarily the best work. The already issues of the Academia world that existed before AI will just augment. It is clear that conferences and journals to keep their reputation will have to do something about AI reviews and overall AI usage. The whole point of research is to increase human knowledge, and today it feels more about a race of who submitted what first, and submit a paper that has more AI usage that brain-cells usage.

We will see that the smartest people won't longer be in Academia, they will probably be in the normal 9-5 work industry, and use AI as a tool on their spear time to help them understand problems and concepts faster as intended without having the pressure to submit a paper.
